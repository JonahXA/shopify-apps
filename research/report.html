The call

TARGET 1Shopify → Accounting reconciliationprimary

The finance niche has the App Store's lowest average rating among high-willingness-to-pay categories (4.54 avg, 88% of apps paid, tiers to $200/mo). Even Intuit's own QuickBooks connector (3,152 reviews) draws a steady stream of concrete, fixable complaints: foreign-currency orders sync wrong amounts, sales tax isn't recorded, and payout-vs-transaction handling makes the books not reconcile. That's a correctness problem in a category merchants pay real money for.

TARGET 2Self-serve store-notification / email template editorfaster v1

A lighter, faster-to-ship hedge. The incumbent (Orderly Emails, 3.8) just torched its trust — a March 2026 update permanently deleted merchants' collections — but the deeper read is that AI now makes template generation cheap. The opening is a safe, self-serve, no-write-access transactional-email editor. Lower build risk; also lower moat. Recommended only as the parallel/backup bet.

Recommendation: Build Target 1 as the flagship attempt and treat its integration surface (QuickBooks/Xero APIs, correctness) as the real work. Keep Target 2 scoped as the fallback if Target 1's build stretches past three weeks.

The finances niche is the sweet spot: high willingness to pay, high dissatisfaction, and — crucially — the pain is data correctness, which is entirely within a solo builder's control. You're not fighting a platform; you're doing the reconciliation math right where incumbents do it wrong.

What merchants actually complain about

Foreign-currency orders sync the wrong amount. “Syncing works fine with US orders, but any Shopify order in a foreign currency will send incorrect price data.”QuickBooks Online · Jul 13 2026 · 1★
Sales tax silently isn't recorded. “The app recognizes the deposits, but it doesn't record the sales tax. Why would an accounting company make an app that doesn't record sales tax on the transactions.”QuickBooks Online · May 15 2026 · 1★
Payouts vs. individual transactions → books don't reconcile. “Gross amounts were showing lower than net amounts, fees were missing, and several transactions were not categorizing or reconciling correctly.”QuickBooks Online · May 13 2026 · 1★
Forced legacy → new migration means rebuilding workflows. “I was notified the old integration was no longer supported… I'll have to rebuild the workflows I'd established on the legacy version.”QuickBooks Online · Jul 2026 · 3★
Onboarding only works with a support agent on the line. Nearly every 5★ review names a specific human who set it up for them — a self-serve failure hiding inside a good rating.Pattern across QuickBooks Online reviews
The wedge: a bookkeeping sync that gets multi-currency, sales tax, and payout reconciliation right by default, with self-serve setup and a visible "your books match to the cent" reconciliation view. Not more features — the same feature, correct.

v1 feature spec — the minimum that fixes the top complaints

In scope

  • One-way sync: Shopify orders/refunds/payouts → QuickBooks Online (start with QBO only)
  • Correct multi-currency: post in store currency, record the FX gain/loss line
  • Sales tax mapped to a proper tax line/liability account, per jurisdiction
  • Payout-level reconciliation: match a Shopify payout to its bank deposit, with fees split out — so the books tie to the cent
  • A reconciliation dashboard: "N orders synced, $X matched, 0 unreconciled"
  • Fully self-serve OAuth onboarding + a mapping wizard (accounts, tax, currencies)

Explicitly out of scope for v1

  • Xero, Sage, other ledgers (add after QBO proves out)
  • Two-way / inventory sync
  • Historical backfill beyond 60 days
  • Multi-store consolidation
  • Anything requiring a support call — if it needs a human, it's a bug

Two-week build plan

Assumes Shopify CLI + the official Remix app template, hosted on a single Node service. The correctness work — not the plumbing — is where the time goes.

Week 1 — plumbing & sync

  1. Scaffold Remix app via Shopify CLI; dev store; OAuth + billing API wired
  2. QuickBooks OAuth; store tokens; sandbox company connected
  3. Ingest Shopify orders/refunds via webhooks + a backfill job
  4. Map an order → QBO sales receipt; get one clean write end-to-end
  5. Multi-currency: post store currency + FX line; test 3 currencies

Week 2 — correctness & ship

  1. Sales-tax mapping to tax/liability accounts, per jurisdiction
  2. Payout reconciliation: match payout → deposit, split fees; tie to cent
  3. Reconciliation dashboard + mapping wizard (self-serve setup)
  4. Billing gates, trial, error states, empty states, docs pages
  5. Test-store dress rehearsal; listing copy + screenshots; submit for review
Honest risk: accounting is correctness-critical and the QBO API has real surface area — this may run 3 weeks, not 2, and a wrong number erodes trust fast. That's precisely why it's defensible: incumbents get it wrong and it's hard to get right. If week 2 slips, ship Target 2 first to keep the portfolio cadence, and let this one take the time it needs.

Target 2 — Safe self-serve transactional-email editor

The faster, lower-risk bet — and the lower-moat one. Orderly Emails (3.8★, 626 reviews) is the wounded incumbent: a March 2026 update permanently deleted merchants' collections, and the 1★ reviews are a wall of merchants who lost years of SEO with no restore path and a terse "oops, sorry." The opening is a template editor that never asks for write access to store data and generates clean Liquid you own.

An update deleted store data with no restore. “A bug in an Orderly Emails update permanently deleted multiple collections… the data could not be recovered — neither by the app developers nor by Shopify.”Orderly Emails · Apr–Jul 2026 · 1★ (multiple merchants)

Method & honesty notes

  • Data: scraped the App Store's server-rendered category pages (52 leaf categories, ~40 apps each) for handle/name/rating/review-count/pricing, plus 222 listing pages for launch dates and price tiers, plus targeted 1–2★ review mining. Respected robots.txt (avoided /internal/, /services/, q= params) with a 1.2s crawl delay and on-disk caching. Pipeline committed to ~/Documents/GitHub/shopify-apps.
  • Known gap: review pagination is JavaScript-driven, so static scraping caps at ~10 reviews per rating filter per app. Complaint themes above come from the most-recent low-star reviews plus browser-rendered deep reads of the finalists — directional, not exhaustive. Deeper mining (headless browser) is the obvious next step before committing.
  • What the data does not yet tell us: true install counts (only review counts as a proxy), churn, or actual revenue. Those only come after we ship and watch organic installs — which is exactly what the 60–90 day kill-gate measures.
